Tiered Execution — settings redesign

The Settings → Tiered Execution panel (TieredExecutionSection.tsx, PAN-2684) asks you to invent tier names, spread one decision across two disconnected surfaces, and renders every default at full volume. This proposal replaces the tier abstraction with the question you're actually answering — who works on what? Everything in the proposed section is clickable.

Kind overrides
  • “tier-1, tier-2, tier-3” are names you had to invent, and they mean nothing. The decision you're actually making is “who handles trivial work, who handles expert work” — but the UI makes you define abstract containers first and wire them up second.
  • One decision, two disconnected surfaces. Difficulty→tier lives in a read-only readout at the top AND as checkboxes inside each tier card. Uncheck “medium” in tier-2 and the error appears far away — and unmapped/double-mapped states are perfectly reachable.
  • Defaults shout. Nine kind selects render “Default routing”, and Feed/Escalation/Replay each occupy a full card even when every value is stock.
  • compaction_reroute has no control at all. It's in the config schema, in your live config.yaml, and the docs claim the panel includes it — the only way to change it is hand-editing YAML.
  • No cost signal, no hazard signal. Tiers exist to save money but nothing shows $/1M; and your live config runs Kimi on claude-code — the PAN-1865 CLIProxy deadlock trap — with no warning.

Proposed — who works on what

No more tier names. You assign each difficulty a crew — a model, or a weighted mix — and crews are labeled by what they are (“Kimi K2.7 Code”, “4-model mix”), never by a name you invent. Coverage gaps are impossible: every difficulty always points at a crew. Tier names still exist in the YAML, auto-derived from the difficulties they cover. Your live config imports as just two crews, because tier-2 and tier-3 were identical mixes.

What changed, and why

  
The question replaces the abstraction. “Tiers” forced you to create containers, name them, then wire difficulties to them with checkboxes — with a validator to catch the wiring mistakes. Here each difficulty simply picks its crew, so “every difficulty maps to exactly one tier” isn't a rule the form checks; it's the only thing the form can express. The validator's whole difficulty-coverage class of errors is gone by construction.

  
Crews are labeled by what they are. “Kimi K2.7 Code” and “4-model mix · GPT-5.5 / GLM-5.2 / Kimi / M3” need no legend. Costs ride along ($/1M, blended for mixes) because cheap-work-cheap is the entire point of this feature. Harness defaults to the model's provider routing; an explicit harness that contradicts it gets an amber warning — your live config's Kimi-on-claude-code (the PAN-1865 deadlock trap) would have been flagged the day it was set.

  
Defaults are quiet. Kind overrides show only actual overrides. Feed, escalation, and replay collapse to one-line summaries of their current resolution and expand only when you need them — same overrides-only principle as the issue Policies control. The YAML disclosure keeps the file honest and shows where the auto-derived tier names go.
